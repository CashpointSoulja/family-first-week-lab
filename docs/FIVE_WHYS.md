# 5 Whys

Starting point (public, observable): **families can only join a waiting list for Lilli's direct-to-family product.** I don't know Lilli's internal reasons, so this is my reasoning about the problem any family proposition has to solve, not a diagnosis of Lilli.

1. **Why does a family product need a first week designed with care?** Because paying for and installing sensors only feels worth it once the family gets something useful, and that can't happen on day one.
2. **Why can't it happen on day one?** Because nothing can be collected until the monitored person agrees, and then sensors need to be placed and a day of data gathered before routine means anything.
3. **Why does consent come before everything?** Because the person being monitored is an adult with rights. If they feel watched rather than asked, they may refuse or quietly unplug sensors, and the family relationship takes the hit.
4. **Why is a thin first day risky?** Because early data has gaps (a sensor not paired, a plug switched off). If the product reads gaps as quiet, a family may be falsely reassured. If it reads them as change, they may be falsely alarmed.
5. **Why does that matter for retention?** Because one false reassurance or false alarm teaches the family not to trust the summary, and families who don't trust it stop opening it.

**Root problem to design for:** the first week has to earn trust from two people at once, the monitored person (control) and the family member (honest usefulness), before there is enough data to be useful.
