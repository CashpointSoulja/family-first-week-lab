# Discovery guide

**Status: no interviews have been run.** This is the guide I would use. There are no quotes, findings or participant counts anywhere in this repo.

## Who I'd talk to
- 6–8 family members who are considering or have tried home-monitoring for a relative living alone.
- 4–6 people who live alone and have been asked (or might be asked) to be monitored, interviewed separately and never in front of the family member.
- Recruitment through public channels with informed consent, no incentive pressure, and the option to stop at any time.

## Five neutral questions (family members and carers)
1. "Tell me about the last time you wondered how [they] were getting on. What happened next?"
2. "How do you currently keep in touch day to day, and what works or doesn't about that?"
3. "When you think about [them] sharing information about their routine with you, what feels okay and what doesn't?"
4. "Walk me through how you'd want to hear if something about their routine looked different, or whether you'd want to hear at all."
5. "If you set something like this up, what would make you stop using it?"

Each question avoids naming a product, avoids "would you use", and asks about past behaviour first.

## For people who would be monitored (separate sessions)
Same intent, reframed: "When family worries about you, how does that show up?" and "What would you want to stay private, even from family?"

## What I'd look for
- Whether "fewer worried calls", "notice change" or "coordinate family" is the stronger job (JTBD).
- Reactions to per-category consent wording and the "Not shared" label.
- Whether "no data, this is unknown" reads as honest or alarming (H3).
- Preferred digest frequency, before seeing any option (Experiment 2 input).

## Ethics
No recordings without explicit consent. No health questions. No interviews with people who can't consent for themselves. Notes are anonymised and deleted after synthesis.
